📚 Chapter-wise Notes

  • SI Units: 7 base units — meter, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela
  • Dimensional Analysis: Use [M L T] to check formula validity
  • Significant Figures: All non-zero digits are significant; leading zeros are not
  • Errors: Absolute, relative, and percentage errors in measurements
  • Tip: Practice dimensional analysis problems!
  • Newton's 1st Law: A body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a force
  • Newton's 2nd Law: F = ma (net force = mass × acceleration)
  • Newton's 3rd Law: Every action has an equal and opposite reaction
  • Friction: f = μN — static friction ≥ kinetic friction
  • Free Body Diagrams: Always draw FBDs for complex problems!
  • Stay Focus: Problems on connected bodies, pulleys, and inclined planes
  • Work: W = F · d · cosθ
  • Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv²
  • Potential Energy: PE = mgh (gravitational)
  • Work-Energy Theorem: Net work = Change in KE
  • Conservation of Energy: Total energy in an isolated system is constant
  • Power: P = W/t = F · v
  • Coulomb's Law: F = kq&sub1;q&sub2;/r²
  • Electric Field: E = F/q (force per unit charge)
  • Electric Potential: V = kQ/r
  • Gauss's Law: Φ = q/ε&sub0; (flux through closed surface)
  • Capacitors: C = Q/V, series and parallel combinations
  • Competition Tip: Master electric field diagrams and potential energy concepts
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR
  • Kirchhoff's Laws: Junction rule (current) & Loop rule (voltage)
  • Wheatstone Bridge: Balanced when P/Q = R/S
  • Meter Bridge & Potentiometer: Important for Competition practical questions
  • Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R

  • Must Do Organic Chemistry — GOC, Reactions, Named Reactions
  • Must Do Chemical Bonding & Molecular Structure
  • Must Do Coordination Compounds
  • Important Equilibrium — Ionic, Chemical, pH
  • Important p-Block & d-Block Elements
  • Good to Know Biomolecules, Polymers, Chemistry in Everyday Life

📚 Chapter-wise Notes

  • Bohr's Model: Electrons orbit in fixed energy levels; E = -13.6/n² eV
  • Quantum Numbers: n (principal), l (azimuthal), m (magnetic), s (spin)
  • Aufbau Principle: Electrons fill lowest energy orbitals first
  • Hund's Rule: Every orbital in a subshell gets one electron before pairing
  • Pauli's Exclusion: No two electrons can have same 4 quantum numbers
  • Ionic Bond: Transfer of electrons (high ΔEN)
  • Covalent Bond: Sharing of electrons
  • VSEPR Theory: Predicts molecular geometry from electron pairs
  • Hybridization: sp (linear), sp² (trigonal planar), sp³ (tetrahedral)
  • MOT: Bonding & antibonding molecular orbitals, bond order
  • Hydrogen Bonding: Intermolecular force with F, O, N
  • Inductive Effect: +I (electron donating) and -I (electron withdrawing)
  • Resonance: Delocalization of electrons across conjugated systems
  • Hyperconjugation: No-bond resonance with α-hydrogens
  • Carbocation Stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH&sub3;+
  • Reaction Types: SN1, SN2, E1, E2 — know the conditions!
  • Named Reactions: Wurtz, Aldol, Cannizzaro, Kolbe — memorize!
  • Enthalpy: ΔH = heat change at constant pressure
  • Entropy: ΔS = measure of randomness
  • Gibbs Free Energy: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS (spontaneity check)
  • Equilibrium Constant: K = products/reactants at equilibrium
  • Le Chatelier's Principle: System adjusts to counter applied stress
  • Werner's Theory: Primary (ionizable) & secondary (non-ionizable) valency
  • IUPAC Naming: Ligands (alphabetical) + metal + oxidation state
  • Isomerism: Geometrical (cis/trans), optical, linkage, ionization
  • Crystal Field Theory: t2g and eg splitting, CFSE
  • Magnetic Properties: Paramagnetic vs diamagnetic based on unpaired electrons

📚 Chapter-wise Notes

  • Cell Theory: All living things are made of cells; cell is the basic unit of life
  • Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic: Nucleus presence, membrane-bound organelles
  • Cell Organelles: Mitochondria (powerhouse), ER, Golgi, Lysosomes, Ribosomes
  • Cell Membrane: Fluid mosaic model — phospholipid bilayer + proteins
  • Nucleus: Contains DNA, nuclear envelope with pores, nucleolus
  • Competition Tip: Diagrams are important — practice drawing organelles!
  • Alimentary Canal: Mouth → Esophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine
  • Enzymes: Amylase (starch), Pepsin (protein), Lipase (fats)
  • Stomach: HCl activates pepsinogen to pepsin; pH ~1.5-2
  • Small Intestine: Major absorption site; villi increase surface area
  • Liver: Produces bile for fat emulsification
  • Competition Tip: Know all enzymes and their substrates!
  • Law of Dominance: One allele dominates over the other
  • Law of Segregation: Alleles separate during gamete formation
  • Law of Independent Assortment: Genes on different chromosomes sort independently
  • Monohybrid Cross: 3:1 phenotypic ratio (Tt x Tt)
  • Dihybrid Cross: 9:3:3:1 ratio
  • Incomplete Dominance: Blending (e.g., pink snapdragons)
  • Codominance: Both alleles expressed (e.g., AB blood group)
  • Ecosystem: Biotic + abiotic components interacting together
  • Food Chain: Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary → Tertiary
  • Energy Flow: Only 10% energy transfers to next trophic level
  • Ecological Pyramids: Number, biomass, and energy pyramids
  • Biogeochemical Cycles: Carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, water cycles
  • Biodiversity: α, β, γ diversity; hotspots (Western Ghats!)
  • Light Reaction: Occurs in thylakoids; produces ATP + NADPH + O&sub2;
  • Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle): Occurs in stroma; fixes CO&sub2; into glucose
  • Photosystems: PS-I (P700) and PS-II (P680)
  • C3 vs C4 Plants: C4 has Kranz anatomy, no photorespiration
  • CAM Plants: Fix CO&sub2; at night (succulents, cacti)
  • Factors: Light intensity, CO&sub2; concentration, temperature
